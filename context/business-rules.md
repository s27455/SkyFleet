# SkyFleet — Business Rules & MVP Behavior

This document records business behavior independently of implementation details. Confirmed decisions are authoritative for the MVP. Open questions must not be silently answered by Claude or by implementation assumptions.

## 1. Product and business model

- SkyFleet is a B2B drone-delivery and fleet-operations platform.
- Partners are businesses such as restaurants, fast-food outlets, and retail stores, often located in shopping malls.
- End customers order from a Partner, choose drone delivery, and provide the delivery address to that Partner.
- The Partner submits the delivery request to SkyFleet. The end customer is not a direct SkyFleet platform user in the MVP.
- Orders may be created manually by an operator during early development and generated by the Simulation Worker in the MVP demonstration. There is no Partner-facing portal or customer ordering UI in the MVP.

## 2. Hub rules

- A Hub is both a parcel handover/pickup point and a drone operational base.
- In the MVP, drone storage, charging, inspection, maintenance, and mission preparation take place at Hubs.
- Partners may use any available Hub in the SkyFleet network; they are not permanently restricted to one Hub.
- Every Order identifies the specific Hub where its parcel is handed over and picked up by a drone.
- Initial Hub drone capacity is 50 drones. This is capacity for accommodating drones, not parcels or orders.
- Hub occupancy counts ONLY drones physically present and docked at the Hub (50 places). A drone that takes off stops counting immediately; a drone in flight counts toward no Hub; a drone that lands and docks counts toward the Hub where it docked.
- Mission assignment and cancellation never change physical occupancy. There are no landing-slot reservations: an active mission does not reserve capacity at its planned return Hub.
- Actual physical occupancy must never exceed the Hub's capacity (50). Emergency returns do not exceed it.
- In the MVP, physical presence is approximated from drone status (not in flight) and stored position (within 50 m of the Hub). This is a simulation approximation: future docking and charging hardware will confirm presence, and GPS proximity will not be the authoritative source. `Drone.hub_id` is the Hub where the drone last docked successfully (set on a successful landing, a valid diversion included; never during flight or after an emergency); it is not presence.
- Hubs have no operational status in the MVP; every Hub is treated as operationally available.

## 3. Order and parcel flow

- An incoming Partner request does not by itself mean that the parcel has been handed over.
- A customer deposits the parcel in a partner parcel locker, which initiates the order process; the parcel is then transported to the selected SkyFleet Hub. An order can therefore exist before the parcel physically reaches the Hub. Parcel-locker integration is out of scope for the MVP implementation.
- The existing statuses are read as: `CREATED` — the order exists (the locker deposit may still be pending); `AWAITING_HANDOVER` — the parcel is deposited or on its way to the Hub; `READY_FOR_PICKUP` — the parcel is physically confirmed at the origin Hub and can be assigned to a drone. Only `READY_FOR_PICKUP` orders can be assigned to a mission. A distinct in-transit state may be added later if a feature needs it.
- Assignment is derived from the mission; there is no order `ASSIGNED` status.
- Parcel validation and acceptance checks at the Hub are assumed to have succeeded before the SkyFleet delivery workflow begins; detailed parcel validation is outside the MVP scope.
- The confirmed initial Order states are `CREATED`, `AWAITING_HANDOVER`, and `READY_FOR_PICKUP`.
- The complete Order lifecycle after `READY_FOR_PICKUP` is not yet finalized. Do not invent a definitive exhaustive status enum. Propose the minimum transitions needed for a specific feature and ask only if the missing decision blocks that feature.
- The delivery address is stored directly on the Order for the MVP, together with coordinates when available and optional delivery instructions. No separate DeliveryAddress entity/table is required at this stage.
- A drone arriving at the delivery location waits for the recipient to collect the parcel.
- If the recipient collects the parcel, the delivery is successful and the drone returns without the parcel.
- If the recipient does not collect the parcel (for example, they cannot locate the landing point), the delivery attempt is unsuccessful; the drone retains the parcel and returns to a Hub.

## 4. Drone operations and dispatch

- The backend owns drone state, mission state, telemetry, dispatch decisions, and simulation state.
- Before assigning/launching a mission, the system checks drone availability and simulated operating conditions, including weather and airspace availability.
- Immediately before takeoff, the drone/system performs a second simulated check of weather and airspace availability.
- A simulated restriction, such as an emergency-service flight restriction, may prevent or delay takeoff. This is a simulation scenario only; no real emergency-service or airspace integration is in scope.
- Rain warning (Feature 020, MVP): an operator can activate one global, persisted rain warning. While it is active no NEW flight may start (manual start, dispatch, Auto Dispatch, manual or automatic relocation); the refusal is 409 `RAIN_WARNING_ACTIVE` and writes nothing. Flights already in the air continue normally (return, diversion, landing, recall, failure handling), orders and assigned missions are never cancelled by it, and takeoffs resume when the operator clears it. MVP simplification: it is a manual precautionary state, not weather data.
- After every delivery attempt, the drone returns to the nearest suitable Hub. The return Hub may differ from the pickup Hub.
- After returning, a drone carrying an undelivered parcel is unloaded at the Hub. The drone receives a quick post-flight inspection and then charges, enters maintenance if needed, or waits for another task.
- “Nearest suitable Hub”, as used for mission planning in the MVP: the nearest Hub by great-circle distance (ties go to the lower Hub id) that the drone can reach with the parcel still on board within the distance budget and battery reserve, and that has a free physical place. Suitability beyond that (for example Hub operational status or charging availability) remains open. The planned return Hub is not guaranteed or reserved: flight execution must recheck capacity before landing and, if the planned Hub is no longer available, choose another reachable suitable Hub.
- The dispatcher may rebalance idle, sufficiently charged drones by repositioning them toward Hubs with greater fleet need, to avoid one Hub being crowded while others are under-supplied. Exact thresholds, target distribution, and prioritization are open implementation policies.
- Repositioning must respect Hub capacity. It must not interrupt an active mission.
- Future fleet rebalancing (planned, not implemented): the operational target is about 40 physically present drones per Hub (80% of 50). At or above 40 the system should consider relocating suitable idle drones to less occupied Hubs, aiming to keep about 10 free places. 40 is not a hard landing limit: temporary occupancy above 40 is allowed, never above 50. Rebalancing must consider battery, destination capacity, drone availability and operational demand.

### Mission planning and assignment (MVP)

- An operator previews a plan for one order and one drone, confirms it, and may cancel it before takeoff. Automatic drone selection and dispatch are a later feature. Confirmation recomputes and revalidates the plan (order readiness, drone availability, energy, return Hub) and never trusts an earlier preview.
- The drone must be `IDLE`, physically docked at the order's origin Hub, able to carry the parcel (model payload, 3 kg for the SF-1), and free of any active mission. At most one active mission (`ASSIGNED` or `IN_FLIGHT`) exists per order and per drone, enforced by the database.
- Mission statuses: `ASSIGNED`, `IN_FLIGHT` and `CANCELLED`. `IN_FLIGHT` is reserved for the flight simulation; planning, confirmation and cancellation never start a flight, move a drone or change its battery.
- An `ASSIGNED` mission can be cancelled before takeoff, which makes the drone and the order available again; cancellation does not change Hub occupancy. A mission that is in flight cannot be cancelled this way.
- Order cancellation (Feature 018B, MVP): an operator cancels an order with `POST /api/orders/{id}/cancel`; the order becomes `CANCELLED` and keeps its history and an `ORDER_CANCELLED` event. Before takeoff an `ASSIGNED` mission is cancelled with it. While the drone is flying with the parcel (before the handover) the drone is recalled: it flies back, still carrying the parcel, to the nearest reachable Hub, and lands under the normal landing rules. A delivered order cannot be cancelled, and cancelling never changes a drone's health or overrides an emergency. MVP simplification (not an approved business rule): the parcel stays on the drone and counts as returned when it lands; nothing further is done with it.
- Drone failure (Feature 018C, MVP): an operator trigger makes one eligible flying drone fail. The drone abandons its delivery or recall route, flies on to a simulated emergency landing point about 250 m ahead and stays grounded as `MAINTENANCE` (requires service); its mission ends in `EMERGENCY`, it is never docked, dispatched or charged again automatically, and no repair workflow exists. The order keeps its status (an undelivered order shows as interrupted by the drone failure), stays in the history, and leaves the active views 120 s after the landing. Cancelling such an order is refused with an explanation. MVP simplification (not an approved business rule): the parcel is not handled further and no compensation or reassignment of the order is defined.
- Failed delivery (Feature 018D, MVP): an operator marks a delivery on its way as "recipient unavailable". The drone still flies to the destination; there the handover fails, the order becomes `DELIVERY_FAILED` (never `DELIVERED`) and the drone brings the parcel back to the nearest reachable Hub, still loaded, and lands under the normal rules; it is not put in maintenance. The failure is recorded as one event at the failed handover. A failed delivery stays in the history, leaves the active views 120 s later and cannot be cancelled; a cancellation or a drone failure that happens before the attempt takes precedence. MVP simplification (not an approved business rule): the parcel counts as returned when the drone lands; storage, a second attempt and handing it back to the Partner are not defined.
- LPR helicopter (Feature 018E, MVP simulation, not an aviation safety system): an operator starts one fictional LPR helicopter pass at a time; the helicopter has priority over delivery traffic and a moving safety zone travels with it. Delivery drones that would meet it wait temporarily, standing still at the edge of its corridor, and resume the same mission once it has passed; waiting uses battery like flying. The scenario never cancels, delivers, fails or deletes an order and adds no order status. A drone that cannot afford to wait ends in the existing battery emergency instead of flying through the zone. A drone failure overrides the wait, and a grounded drone never resumes. Simulation limit, not a guaranteed safety behavior: a drone already inside the corridor that cannot leave in time stands still and the zone passes over it. Route, speed, radius and duration are fictional parameters.
- Route: origin Hub, then the delivery destination, then the return Hub chosen as described above (it may be the origin Hub). The total must stay within the model's recommended mission distance (14 km for the SF-1).
- Energy (simulation assumptions, not certified aircraft performance): a drone uses `100 / nominal range` percent of battery per km empty (5% per km for the SF-1's 20 km range). Carrying the parcel multiplies that by `1 + factor × weight / maximum payload`, with a configurable factor (default 0.5). The outbound leg carries the parcel and the return leg does not (expected energy). As a conservative safety check the drone must be able to fly the whole route with the parcel still on board, plus the 20% minimum battery reserve; exactly the required level is sufficient.

## 5. Monitoring, telemetry, and operator controls

- The operator has a map-based live view of Hub locations, drone positions, and operational states.
- Operators can inspect orders, missions/flights, basic statistics, drone states, and recorded events/logs.
- Selecting a drone opens a detailed view. The intended experience includes a 3D drone representation and its current telemetry/health indicators.
- Simulated telemetry may include position, altitude, speed, estimated time to destination, motor temperature, battery temperature, battery level, and other relevant flight/health values.
- Operators should be able to request supported interventions such as canceling/aborting a flight, requesting a return, or requesting a landing. Exact command semantics, allowed states, and safety validation are not yet fully specified; backend validation is required.
- The frontend displays backend-owned state and does not independently decide operational outcomes.

## 6. AI and operational events

- The system records operational logs, events, and telemetry so activity can be inspected and investigated.
- AI analyzes available operational events and incidents and helps notify the operator when something requires attention.
- AI may explain events and recommend actions. Any state-changing action suggested or initiated through AI requires explicit operator approval and backend validation.
- Deterministic business rules (eligibility, dispatch, state transitions, capacity checks) remain outside the LLM.

## 7. MVP simulation boundaries

- Weather, airspace restrictions, drone movement, telemetry, and incidents are simulated.
- Do not add real weather APIs, real air-traffic/airspace integrations, real emergency-service feeds, real drone hardware, or physical flight-control logic.
- The purpose is a credible operational demonstration, not a certified aviation system or physically accurate flight simulator.
- The BPMN diagram documents the main end-to-end business flow. It is not expected to contain every exception, incident, or low-level state transition; this document describes supporting behavior.

## 8. Open questions — do not invent answers

1. **Order lifecycle:** What exact states and transitions follow `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, including assignment, flight, successful delivery, failed delivery, cancellation, and completion? Define only as implementation requires.
2. **Suitable return Hub (partly answered):** For planning, a full Hub (physical occupancy at 50) is skipped and the nearest suitable Hub with a free place is chosen; emergency returns never exceed 50 (see §2 and §4). Still open: suitability beyond distance, reachability and capacity (for example Hub operational status), and what the flight simulation does when no reachable Hub has a free place at landing time.
3. **Returned parcel handling (partly answered for cancellation and failed delivery):** after a cancellation recall or a failed delivery (Feature 018D) the MVP keeps the parcel on the drone until it lands and does nothing further (see §4). Still open: After an unsuccessful delivery and return to a Hub, is the parcel stored, made available for another attempt, or handed back to the Partner? Who/what confirms that handling?
4. **Fleet balancing (partly answered):** The target is about 40 of 50 places occupied per Hub (see §4); exact demand-based prioritization and what counts as “sufficiently charged” for repositioning remain open (planned for the fleet rebalancing feature).
5. **Operator interventions (partly answered):** Cancelling an `ASSIGNED` mission before takeoff is defined (§4); the order stays `READY_FOR_PICKUP`. Cancelling an order in flight now recalls the drone to a Hub (Feature 018B, §4). Still open: separate return-to-Hub and land commands for a flight that has started, and what happens to the Order and parcel after each.
6. **Emergency landing and drone malfunction (partly answered):** Feature 018C simulates a manual drone failure with a simulated emergency landing and a grounded drone that requires service (see §4, MVP simplification). Still open: what happens to the parcel and the interrupted order afterwards (reassignment, recovery, compensation), how a failed drone is repaired and returned to service, and automatic or random failures.
7. **Weather/airspace restrictions (partly answered):** Feature 020 defines a manual global rain warning that suspends new takeoffs until cleared (§4); automatic work is skipped without retries or events while it is active. Still open: other restrictions (airspace, emergency services), any automatic trigger, and retry or delay semantics beyond "wait until cleared".
8. **Hub occupancy semantics — answered:** the 50-drone capacity counts only drones physically present and docked at the Hub; assigned or returning drones are not counted and nothing is reserved (see §2). The MVP approximates presence from status and position; docking hardware will confirm it later.

## 9. Scope discipline for Claude Code

- Treat the confirmed rules above as approved requirements.
- Do not ask again about decisions already recorded here.
- Classify new uncertainties as **Blocker**, **MVP simplification**, or **Future**.
- Ask the user only about a Blocker that prevents the current task from being implemented correctly or safely.
- For an MVP simplification, propose a minimal, reversible assumption and clearly label it for approval; do not silently turn it into a confirmed business rule.
- Record non-blocking future scenarios without stopping the current feature.
- Do not expand a feature's scope to solve every possible operational exception.
