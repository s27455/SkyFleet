# SkyFleet — Business Rules & MVP Behavior

This document records business behavior independently of implementation details. Confirmed decisions are authoritative for the MVP. Open questions must not be silently answered by Claude or by implementation assumptions.

## 1. Product and business model

- SkyFleet is a B2B drone-delivery and fleet-operations platform.
- Partners are businesses such as restaurants, fast-food outlets, and retail stores, often located in shopping malls.
- End customers order from a Partner, choose drone delivery, and provide the delivery address to that Partner.
- The Partner submits the delivery request to SkyFleet. The end customer is not a direct SkyFleet platform user in the MVP.
- Orders may be created manually by an operator during early development and generated by the Simulation Worker in the MVP demonstration. There is no Partner-facing portal or customer ordering UI in the MVP.

## 2. Hub rules

- A Hub is both a parcel handover/pickup point and a drone operational base.
- In the MVP, drone storage, charging, inspection, maintenance, and mission preparation take place at Hubs.
- Partners may use any available Hub in the SkyFleet network; they are not permanently restricted to one Hub.
- Every Order identifies the specific Hub where its parcel is handed over and picked up by a drone.
- Initial Hub drone capacity is 50 drones. This is capacity for accommodating drones, not parcels or orders.
- Ordinary placement/repositioning must respect the Hub's capacity. Exceptional return behavior when a Hub is full remains open.

## 3. Order and parcel flow

- An incoming Partner request does not by itself mean that the parcel has been handed over.
- Parcel validation and acceptance checks at the Hub are assumed to have succeeded before the SkyFleet delivery workflow begins; detailed parcel validation is outside the MVP scope.
- The confirmed initial Order states are `CREATED`, `AWAITING_HANDOVER`, and `READY_FOR_PICKUP`.
- The complete Order lifecycle after `READY_FOR_PICKUP` is not yet finalized. Do not invent a definitive exhaustive status enum. Propose the minimum transitions needed for a specific feature and ask only if the missing decision blocks that feature.
- The delivery address is stored directly on the Order for the MVP, together with coordinates when available and optional delivery instructions. No separate DeliveryAddress entity/table is required at this stage.
- A drone arriving at the delivery location waits for the recipient to collect the parcel.
- If the recipient collects the parcel, the delivery is successful and the drone returns without the parcel.
- If the recipient does not collect the parcel (for example, they cannot locate the landing point), the delivery attempt is unsuccessful; the drone retains the parcel and returns to a Hub.

## 4. Drone operations and dispatch

- The backend owns drone state, mission state, telemetry, dispatch decisions, and simulation state.
- Before assigning/launching a mission, the system checks drone availability and simulated operating conditions, including weather and airspace availability.
- Immediately before takeoff, the drone/system performs a second simulated check of weather and airspace availability.
- A simulated restriction, such as an emergency-service flight restriction, may prevent or delay takeoff. This is a simulation scenario only; no real emergency-service or airspace integration is in scope.
- After every delivery attempt, the drone returns to the nearest suitable Hub. The return Hub may differ from the pickup Hub.
- After returning, a drone carrying an undelivered parcel is unloaded at the Hub. The drone receives a quick post-flight inspection and then charges, enters maintenance if needed, or waits for another task.
- The exact meaning of “nearest suitable Hub” is not fully defined. Distance is relevant, but suitability constraints and tie-breaking rules remain open.
- The dispatcher may rebalance idle, sufficiently charged drones by repositioning them toward Hubs with greater fleet need, to avoid one Hub being crowded while others are under-supplied. Exact thresholds, target distribution, and prioritization are open implementation policies.
- Repositioning must respect Hub capacity. It must not interrupt an active mission.

## 5. Monitoring, telemetry, and operator controls

- The operator has a map-based live view of Hub locations, drone positions, and operational states.
- Operators can inspect orders, missions/flights, basic statistics, drone states, and recorded events/logs.
- Selecting a drone opens a detailed view. The intended experience includes a 3D drone representation and its current telemetry/health indicators.
- Simulated telemetry may include position, altitude, speed, estimated time to destination, motor temperature, battery temperature, battery level, and other relevant flight/health values.
- Operators should be able to request supported interventions such as canceling/aborting a flight, requesting a return, or requesting a landing. Exact command semantics, allowed states, and safety validation are not yet fully specified; backend validation is required.
- The frontend displays backend-owned state and does not independently decide operational outcomes.

## 6. AI and operational events

- The system records operational logs, events, and telemetry so activity can be inspected and investigated.
- AI analyzes available operational events and incidents and helps notify the operator when something requires attention.
- AI may explain events and recommend actions. Any state-changing action suggested or initiated through AI requires explicit operator approval and backend validation.
- Deterministic business rules (eligibility, dispatch, state transitions, capacity checks) remain outside the LLM.

## 7. MVP simulation boundaries

- Weather, airspace restrictions, drone movement, telemetry, and incidents are simulated.
- Do not add real weather APIs, real air-traffic/airspace integrations, real emergency-service feeds, real drone hardware, or physical flight-control logic.
- The purpose is a credible operational demonstration, not a certified aviation system or physically accurate flight simulator.
- The BPMN diagram documents the main end-to-end business flow. It is not expected to contain every exception, incident, or low-level state transition; this document describes supporting behavior.

## 8. Open questions — do not invent answers

1. **Order lifecycle:** What exact states and transitions follow `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, including assignment, flight, successful delivery, failed delivery, cancellation, and completion? Define only as implementation requires.
2. **Suitable return Hub:** What makes a Hub suitable beyond geographic proximity? What happens if the nearest Hub is at its 50-drone capacity? Can emergency returns exceed normal capacity, and how should that be represented?
3. **Returned parcel handling:** After an unsuccessful delivery and return to a Hub, is the parcel stored, made available for another attempt, or handed back to the Partner? Who/what confirms that handling?
4. **Fleet balancing:** What thresholds or target distribution determine that a Hub has too many or too few drones? What counts as “sufficiently charged” for repositioning?
5. **Operator interventions:** What exact states allow cancel, return-to-Hub, or land commands? What happens to the Order and parcel after each intervention?
6. **Emergency landing and drone malfunction:** What simplified outcomes should the MVP simulate, and how does the drone/mission/order state change afterward?
7. **Weather/airspace restrictions:** What simplified statuses or retry behavior should be used when pre-flight conditions are not acceptable?
8. **Hub occupancy semantics:** Does the 50-drone capacity count drones physically present at the Hub only, or also drones assigned to/returning to it? Define before implementing concurrency-sensitive capacity logic.

## 9. Scope discipline for Claude Code

- Treat the confirmed rules above as approved requirements.
- Do not ask again about decisions already recorded here.
- Classify new uncertainties as **Blocker**, **MVP simplification**, or **Future**.
- Ask the user only about a Blocker that prevents the current task from being implemented correctly or safely.
- For an MVP simplification, propose a minimal, reversible assumption and clearly label it for approval; do not silently turn it into a confirmed business rule.
- Record non-blocking future scenarios without stopping the current feature.
- Do not expand a feature's scope to solve every possible operational exception.
